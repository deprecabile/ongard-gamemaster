# Ecosphere of Ondgard

## Atmosphere

Ondgard's atmosphere is dense and "heavy", designed to mitigate the thermal excursion of a 32-hour day and retain the
heat of an orange star.

### Composition: Noble-Gas-Enriched Atmosphere

The massive, geologically active planet has accumulated over billions of years a high concentration of **Krypton** (Kr).
This heavy noble gas, together with elevated CO₂ from volcanism and water vapour, creates a dense atmosphere with
excellent thermal buffering.

* **Surface Pressure:** 1.4 atm
* **Density:** ~1.75 times Earth's (due to higher pressure + higher mean molecular weight)
* **Mean Molecular Weight:** 37.2 g/mol (vs 29 g/mol on Earth)

| Element                     | % Vol   | Partial Pressure ($pp$) | Function                                                                                                        |
|:----------------------------|:--------|:------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Nitrogen ($N_2$)**        | **67%** | 0.938 atm               | Inert base gas.                                                                                                 |
| **Oxygen ($O_2$)**          | **15%** | **0.210 atm**           | **Perfectly breathable.** The $ppO_2$ is identical to Earth's (0.21 atm).                                       |
| **Krypton ($Kr$)**          | **15%** | 0.210 atm               | Heavy noble gas (84 g/mol) that drastically increases atmospheric density.                                      |
| **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)** | **2%**  | 0.028 atm               | Enhanced greenhouse effect to retain heat during the long night. Elevated level due to active volcanism.        |
| **Water Vapour ($H_2O$)**   | **1%**  | 0.014 atm               | Constant humidity, additional greenhouse effect and thermal buffer via latent heat of condensation/evaporation. |

### Sensory Effects

1. **Facilitated Flight:** The combination of **high air density** (greater lift) and **lower gravity** (0.8 g,
   reducing weight) makes flight extremely efficient, supporting giant flying creatures and enabling take-offs in
   confined spaces.
2. **Acoustics:** Sound is noticeably deeper and more resonant. Krypton lowers the pitch of the voice (the opposite of
   helium), making all voices "deeper" and fuller.
3. **"Heavy" Air:** The 1.75× density is physically perceptible. Walking against the wind requires more effort, but the
   air also offers greater resistance to rapid movements.

### The Sky of Ondgard

Ondgard's sky displays unique hues owing to the atmospheric composition and the orange starlight.

* **Daytime (Zenith):** The sky takes on a **pale blue** tending toward **grey-blue**, more subdued than Earth's blue.
  The orange star emits less blue radiation than the Sun, so even with the dense atmosphere and enhanced Rayleigh
  scattering, the sky is less saturated.
* **Daytime (Horizon):** At the horizon the sky turns **intense orange** and **golden amber**, the result of multiple
  scattering of the orange light through the dense atmosphere.
* **At Sunset:** Colours blaze into shades of **garnet, deep violet and crimson** as the orange sun sinks slowly below
  the horizon.
* **At Night:** Northern and southern auroras dance across the sky in spectacular streaks of **electric blue, violet,
  green and pink**, produced by the interaction of stellar winds with the atmospheric gases.

## Hydrosphere

The hydrosphere covers **65% of the surface**. Although less than Earth's coverage (71%), on a planet of this size
(radius ~7,800 km) it still guarantees an immense ocean surface (~500 million km²), essential for life.

### Thermal Function: The Great Flywheel

In a world with a 32-hour day, the oceans play a critical role as a **thermal buffer**.

* **Absorption:** During the long daylight hours, water absorbs the energy of the orange star without overheating
  (high specific heat capacity).
* **Release:** During the 16 hours of night, the oceans release heat slowly, preventing atmospheric temperatures from
  plummeting. Without this water mass, the thermal excursion would be lethal.

### The Dichotomy of the Seas

The continental configuration (grouped in a ring-shaped super-archipelago or fragmented so as to shelter inner basins)
creates a sharp division in the waters, fundamental for navigation and habitability.

#### 1. The Outer Ocean ("Il Vasto")

Il Vasto is the open ocean surrounding the landmasses.

* **Characteristics:** Deep, dark, exposed to the full fury of Shinosa's tides.
* **The Danger:** Here, wave inertia can freely add to the gravitational impulses of the moons. Rogue waves and
  unpredictable drift currents form. Il Vasto is biologically rich (vast banks of algae and megafauna) but hostile to
  standard navigation.

#### 2. The Inner Seas

Large interconnected basins enclosed between the landmasses, linked to Il Vasto by natural straits.

* **Protection:** The straits act as "low-pass filters" for waves: they damp the kinetic energy of the violent outer-
  ocean tides. Inside, water levels fluctuate (tides), but without the violence of oceanic rogue waves.
* **Habitability:** It is on these shores that civilisation thrives. The waters are calm, trade routes safe and fishing
  abundant.
* **Salinity:** Slightly lower than Il Vasto due to the input from great continental rivers and less turbulent
  evaporation.

### The "Gentle" Hydrological Cycle

The combination of dense atmosphere (1.4 atm) and reduced gravity (0.8 g) alters the perception of rain.

* **Slow Rain:** Raindrops, encountering greater aerodynamic drag and less gravity, reach a lower terminal velocity
  than on Earth. Downpours, even heavy in water volume, fall with an almost hypnotic "slowness" and strike the ground
  with less force, reducing erosion and favouring absorption by the flora.
* **Absence of Sea Ice:** Thanks to heat redistribution, the sea almost never freezes, except in small stagnant bays
  beyond the 80th parallel.

## Climate System

The climate is dominated by the need to distribute heat during the long rotation (32 hours). The slower rotation
weakens the Coriolis force, allowing warm equatorial air to push further north before cooling and descending.

* **Reduced Coriolis Effect:** The Coriolis force is proportional to the planet's angular rotation speed.
    * **Earth:** ω⊕ = 2π/24h = 7.27 × 10⁻⁵ rad/s → F_Coriolis = 2m·ω⊕·v
    * **Ondgard:** ω_Ond = 2π/32h = 5.45 × 10⁻⁵ rad/s → F_Coriolis = 2m·ω_Ond·v
    * **Ratio:** F_Ond / F⊕ = ω_Ond / ω⊕ = **0.75** (25% reduction)

  This reduction allows air masses to travel further in the meridional direction before being deflected, expanding the
  convective cells.

* **Effect on Climate Belts:** The *Hadley Cell* (tropical) widens significantly. Temperate belts are pushed toward the
  poles. Polar caps shrink drastically.

### Climate Belts

| Belt                           | Latitude    | Climate & Biome                                                                                                                                                         |
|:-------------------------------|:------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Extended Equatorial**        | **0°–35°**  | **Disturbed Tropical.** Windy savannas and immense jungles. No sharp deserts at 30° (like the Sahara) because moisture travels further. Constant but broad trade winds. |
| **Subtropical / Transitional** | **35°–55°** | **The "Golden Belt".** Expanded Mediterranean/Californian climate. Rainy, mild winters (10 °C), warm summers without excess (26 °C). The planet's breadbasket.          |
| **Upper Temperate**            | **55°–75°** | **Oceanic/Boreal.** Cool, humid, dominated by great forests. Similar to Northern Europe/Canada but less frigid in winter thanks to Krypton's thermal inertia.           |
| **Reduced Polar Cap**          | **75°–90°** | **Cold Tundra.** Permanent ice only in deep inland areas or highlands. The sea can remain navigable for most of the year ("Northwest Passage" always open).             |

### Local Dynamics

#### Thermal Inertia: The Secret of Stability

Ondgard's atmosphere has a **total heat capacity 50% greater than Earth's** (1.5×), achieved through:

1. **High atmospheric mass:** 1.4 atm pressure + 1.75× density = far more gas to heat/cool
2. **Enhanced greenhouse effect:** CO₂ at 2% (50× terrestrial levels) traps infrared radiation during the night
3. **Water vapour:** The condensation/evaporation cycle absorbs and releases enormous amounts of latent heat
4. **65% ocean coverage:** The primary thermal buffer, working in synergy with the dense atmosphere

Thanks to this combination, the atmosphere accumulates heat slowly during the day and releases it very slowly during
the night.

#### Day/Night Cycle

* **Reduced Thermal Excursion:** Despite 16 hours of darkness, temperature varies differently by zone:
    * **Coastal Zones:** 8–10 °C excursion (oceanic buffer + dense atmosphere)
    * **Desert Interior:** 15–20 °C excursion (no water masses, but still lower than terrestrial deserts thanks to the
      dense atmosphere)
* **Peaks:** Peak heat occurs in the late afternoon (15:00 local); peak cold just before dawn.

### Winds and Meteorology: The World That Pushes

Atmospheric conditions create a wind unique in its nature, characterised by physical force but contained speed.

* **Impact Law:** Since the air is 1.75 times denser than on Earth, **wind pushes harder at the same speed**.
* A 20 km/h breeze on Ondgard exerts the pressure of a 26 km/h wind on Earth.
* *Effect:* Walking against the wind is physically tiring; ship sails can be smaller and still achieve the same thrust.
* **Fluid Inertia:** The Krypton-rich (heavy gas) air is "dense". It takes a long time to accelerate (no frantic
  gusts) and a long time to stop.
* *Result:* Winds are constant, majestic and predictable. Once a storm starts blowing, it lasts long but without
  sudden changes of direction.
* **Broadened Storms:** Owing to the slow rotation (weak Coriolis), tight, extremely violent cyclones (compact
  hurricanes) do not form. Disturbances are instead **immense systems** covering thousands of kilometres, rotating
  lazily and bringing constant rain for days, without ever reaching destructive wind peaks.

### Precipitation Phenomena

The planet's physics (0.8 g gravity + 1.6× air density) drastically alters the fall of heavy objects, including solid
water.

* **Snow ("The White Velvet"):** In the *Upper Temperate Belt* and on highlands, snow is common but peculiar.
    * **Giant Flakes:** Low gravity and high air resistance allow crystals to aggregate into enormous flakes (as wide
      as a palm) that descend with extreme slowness.
    * **Acoustic Effect:** A snowfall on Ondgard is not just silent — it is a near-perfect sound absorber.
    * **Temperature:** Owing to the thermal inertia of the dense atmosphere and the CO₂ greenhouse effect, dry freezes
      are rare. Snow is often heavy, wet and tends to compact quickly.

* **Hail ("The Slowed Stones"):** A feared, partly counter-intuitive phenomenon.
    * **Genesis:** The dense air provides superior buoyancy. Updrafts can support hailstones far longer.
    * **Size:** Common stones reach 3–5 cm in diameter. During exceptional storms, stones of 8–12 cm (orange-sized)
      can form before overcoming the updraft and falling.
    * **Impact:** Terminal velocity is reduced by 30–40% compared to Earth (0.8 g gravity partially offset by 1.75×
      air density). They do not "shoot" like high-speed projectiles, yet they still carry dangerous kinetic energy.
    * **Damage:** The main danger is **crushing** and blunt impact rather than perforation. The largest stones can cause
      trauma, damage lightweight structures and accumulate dangerously on rooftops. Sheltering under solid cover is
      essential during intense hailstorms.

### Electromagnetic Climate (Space Weather)

The interaction between the magnetic moon Shinosa (1.3 Tesla) and the dense atmosphere creates a visible, tangible
"space weather".

* **Multicoloured Auroras:** Excitation of atmospheric gases produces spectacular auroras: **electric blue and violet**
  (Krypton), **green and red** (Oxygen), **pink** (Nitrogen). These auroras are not confined to the poles but follow
  the moon's passage, creating "highways of light" in the night sky.
* **Dry Storms:** During perigee, the potential difference between the ionosphere and the ground exceeds the
  dielectric strength of the dense air. **Clear-sky lightning** (vertical and silent before the thunder) strikes
  without the need for clouds.
* **Sensory Blackouts:** The magnetic chaos temporarily disorients migratory animals and renders compasses unusable for
  2–3 hours as the satellite passes.
