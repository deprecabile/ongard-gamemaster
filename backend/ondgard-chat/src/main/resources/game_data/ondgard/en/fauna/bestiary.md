# Bestiary of Ondgard

---

## Legendary Creatures

The creatures described here are not part of the documented, observable fauna. They belong to oral tradition, travellers' tales and ancient chronicles. Their existence is a matter of debate: some scholars consider them real but extremely rare, others dismiss them as myths born from popular exaggeration. For the Game Master, these creatures inhabit a narrative grey area — they may be real, extinct, or pure legend, depending on the needs of the story.

### Dragon (Ignevora)

* **Status:** Not sighted for generations. Existence disputed.
* **Size (tradition):** 15–25 m wingspan, 8–20 tonnes. The oldest accounts speak of even larger specimens.
* **Appearance (tradition):** Enormous armoured flying reptile, distinct from common draconiformes (such as the Velarca) by its vastly greater bulk, thick overlapping scale armour resistant to arrows and blades, and a massive horned head. Four clawed limbs in addition to membranous wings. Colouration varies across accounts: black, dark red, bronze.
* **The Fire Breath:** The most feared and debated trait. Natural philosophers who give credence to the accounts hypothesise a two-component biochemical mechanism, analogous to bombardier beetles but on a vastly greater scale, made plausible by Ondgard's specific conditions:
    * **Primary gland (fuel):** Produces and stores **carbon disulphide (CS₂)** in a dense lipid solution. CS₂ has an extremely low flash point (-30°C) and autoignition at just 90°C. On Ondgard, where atmospheric CO₂ at 2% drives a vastly accelerated carbon cycle and active volcanism makes sulphur abundant in the ecosystem, a symbiotic metabolic pathway (specialised sulphur-reducing bacteria within the glands) capable of synthesising CS₂ is biologically plausible. The lipid solution acts as an adhesive carrier: the liquid sticks to surfaces and continues to burn, akin to Greek fire but of biological origin.
    * **Secondary gland (igniter):** Produces **diphosphane (P₂H₄)**, a pyrophoric compound that ignites spontaneously on contact with air. Stored in an anaerobic environment within the gland, separated from the main fuel.
    * **Expulsion mechanism:** The two secretions mix only at the moment of expulsion through the jaws. The diphosphane ignites instantly upon contact with Ondgard's dense atmosphere (1.75 atm, with a higher concentration of O₂ per unit volume), setting off the lipid CS₂ which atomises into fine droplets — the high atmospheric pressure promotes more efficient nebulisation, producing a jet of incendiary liquid fire with a flamethrower effect.
    * Folk chronicles describe "sparks between the teeth" just before the jet: likely the visual interpretation of the diphosphane flashing upon contact with air an instant before the main fuel ignites.
* **Habitat (tradition):** Remote mountain ranges, deep caverns in high peaks, extinct volcanoes. Accounts invariably place dragons in inaccessible locations far from inhabited areas.
* **Behaviour (tradition):** Solitary and territorial. High animal intelligence, perhaps comparable to the great apes. Apex hunter with no natural rivals. Some legends attribute near-sentient intelligence to dragons, but this is generally regarded as narrative embellishment.
* **Last credible sighting:** The most recent chronicles mentioning dragons date back several generations and are already second- or third-hand. No verifiable sighting in modern times.
* **The debate:**
    * **Believers** cite ancient chronicles, giant bones occasionally found in mountain caverns, and the consistency of accounts across cultures that had no contact with one another.
    * **Sceptics** argue that "dragon bones" are fossil remains of exceptionally large common draconiformes, and that the tales have inflated over time. The Velarca and the Grandala, already impressive, would provide the seed from which the legend grew.
* **GM notes:** The dragon is a powerful narrative element precisely because its existence is uncertain. A village living in terror of a dragon might be right — or might have mistaken an unusually large Grandala for something worse. Bones found in a cavern may be definitive proof or a misinterpreted fossil. The choice is the storyteller's.
