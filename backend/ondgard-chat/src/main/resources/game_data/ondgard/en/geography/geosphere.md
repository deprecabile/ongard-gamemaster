# Geosphere of Ondgard

## A "Light" Giant

Ondgard defies standard classifications. Despite its imposing mass (**+22% compared to Earth**), its mean density
(**3.65 g/cm³**) is markedly lower than Earth's (**5.51 g/cm³**).
The explanation lies not in hollow spaces or magic, but in a **unique chemical differentiation**: while Earth is a
planet of iron silicates and heavy metals, Ondgard is a world dominated by light silicates, hydrates and a core rich in
volatile elements.

---

## Internal Structure

### 1. LEE Core — Light Element Enriched (The Chemical Dynamo)

**Radius:** ~4,050 km (~52% of the planetary radius)
**Mass:** ~26.6% of the total mass (1.94 × 10²⁴ kg)
**Density:** 6.79 g/cm³

At the planet's centre pulses an anomalous core, a legacy of formation in a volatile-rich nebula.

#### Chemical Composition:

* **58% Iron-Nickel-Cobalt (Fe-Ni-Co):** The base metallic alloy — conductive and magnetic.
* **21% Silicon (Si):** Lowers density and improves electronic mobility.
* **11% Carbon (C):** Increases thermal conductivity and convection.
* **10% Oxygen (O):** Dissolved in liquid iron at extreme pressure, violently expelled during inner-core
  crystallisation.

#### Two-Layer Structure:

* **Solid Inner Core (Radius ~1,150 km):** Crystallised Fe-Ni-Co alloy, depleted in light elements. Grows slowly
  through secular cooling.
* **Liquid Outer Core (Thickness ~2,900 km):** Molten alloy rich in light elements. This is where the magnetic dynamo
  operates.

#### The Enhanced Magnetic Dynamo:

The massive presence of light elements (42% of the core!) drives **vigorous** convection:

* **Compositional Buoyancy:** During inner-core crystallisation, Si, C and O are continuously expelled upward,
  generating convective currents 2.0 times more intense than Earth's.
* **High Conductivity:** The Fe-Ni-Co-Si alloy maintains an electrical conductivity equal to 70% of Earth's pure
  iron-nickel.
* **Result:** A magnetic field equal to **75% of Earth's** (~37 µT at the surface), more than sufficient to protect
  the dense atmosphere from Kepler's stellar winds and resist the magnetic perturbations of Shinosa.

---

### 2. The Hydrated Mantle (The Geological Sponge)

**Thickness:** ~3,744 km
**Mass:** ~68% of the total mass
**Density:** 3.1 g/cm³

The mantle is not composed of Earth's dense peridotites but of a mixture of **hydrated rocks** (such as serpentinites)
and volatile-rich minerals trapped at high pressure.

#### Characteristics:

* **Intermediate Density:** At roughly **3.0–3.2 g/cm³**, this mantle is lighter than Earth's but sufficiently solid
  to support the crust.
* **Volatile Reservoir:** The crystalline structure of the rocks traps immense quantities of water (H₂O), carbon
  dioxide (CO₂) and noble gases (Krypton). It is a "petrified ocean" that releases its gases through volcanism.
* **Reduced Viscosity:** Water trapped in the minerals lowers the mantle's viscosity, making it more "fluid" and
  facilitating plate tectonics.
* **Thermal Convection:** Radiogenic heat (Uranium, Thorium, Potassium) and residual heat from planetary formation
  keep the mantle in slow convection, fuelling surface volcanism.

#### Transition Zone (400–700 km depth):

A band rich in high-pressure minerals (wadsleyite, ringwoodite) capable of holding up to 3% water by weight. This
"hidden reservoir" is estimated at **2–3 times the mass of the surface oceans**.

### 3. "Thin and Light" Crust (SIAL)

**Mean Thickness:** ~30 km (Continental: 35–40 km, Oceanic: 15–20 km)
**Mass:** ~5.4% of the total mass
**Density:** 2.8 g/cm³

The planetary crust is the key element for isostatic stability and mineral wealth.

#### Felsic Composition:

Dominated by rocks rich in **Silicon and Aluminium** (SIAL), such as:

* **Light Granites:** Intrusive rocks rich in quartz, feldspar and mica.
* **Anorthosites:** Plutonic rocks rich in plagioclase (aluminium silicates).
* **Pumices and Tuffs:** Light, porous volcanic rocks produced by explosive volcanism.
* **Low in Heavy Metals:** Iron, magnesium and dense metals are rare, concentrated in the mantle and core.

#### Structural Characteristics:

* **Thin and Flexible:** The crust is on average thinner than Earth's (30 km versus 35–50 km). This makes it flexible
  and capable of deforming gradually without stress accumulation, drastically reducing seismic risk.
* **Isostatic Equilibrium:** At 0.8 g gravity, the pressure at the base of the crust (~450 MPa) is lower than Earth's,
  allowing the thin crust to float stably on the mantle.
* **Moderate Fragmentation:** The crust is divided into a moderate number of tectonic plates (15 major plates, slightly
  more than Earth), with mean sizes comparable to or larger than Earth's. The greater planetary surface and vigorous
  mantle convection create broad convective cells, favouring large, stable plates rather than excessive fragmentation.

## Surface Geology

The combination of reduced gravity, a lubricated mantle and a thin crust creates a geologically active yet gentle
world, where tectonics and volcanism shape the landscape without catastrophe.

### Lubricated Tectonics ("Gentle Sliding")

Ondgard's tectonic plates, floating on a hydrated mantle with reduced viscosity, move at speeds comparable to Earth's
(2–10 cm/year), but with far more gradual and stable dynamics.

#### Effects:

* **Rare and Moderate Seismic Activity:** The combination of reduced gravity (0.8 g), thin crust and lubricated
  mantle favours **asynchronous creep** along faults. Tectonic stress is released gradually without accumulation,
  preventing major earthquakes. Perceptible seismic events are rare (a few per year per region) and of low intensity
  (M 4–6 maximum). Many correlate with Shinosa's close passages, which rhythmically flex the crust every 45 hours,
  creating small, predictable events.
* **Diffuse Orogenesis:** Mountain ranges are not concentrated in a few zones (Himalayas, Andes) but distributed
  across the entire planet in lower, rounded ridges.
* **Gradual Subduction:** Thin oceanic plates (15–20 km) are subducted easily and without violence, recycling water
  and volatiles into the mantle. Subduction zones produce diffuse volcanism but rarely destructive earthquakes.

### Diffuse and Gentle Volcanism

Owing to the thin crust and volatile-rich mantle, magma rises easily to the surface. There are no few supervolcanoes
but rather **thousands of small eruptive centres**.

#### Characteristics:

* **Constant Degassing:** Volcanoes function as "exhaust pipes" for the mantle, continuously pumping CO₂, water vapour
  and Krypton into the atmosphere, maintaining the elevated surface pressure (1.4 atm).
* **Predominantly Effusive Volcanism:** The low viscosity of the magma (iron-rich and silica-poor) and reduced gravity
  favour **effusive** eruptions (flowing lava) over explosive ones. Eruptions are spectacular but seldom dangerous:
  slow, predictable lava rivers, lava fountains, lava lakes. Explosive eruptions (Vesuvius type) are rare and limited
  to gas-rich magmas in subduction zones.
* **Hotspots:** Mantle plumes rise from depths of 1,000–2,000 km, creating chains of volcanic islands and basalt
  plateaux. Eruptions are continuous but gradual, slowly building enormous shield volcanoes (Hawaii type).

#### Orography:

Mountains grow rapidly from tectonic push but are equally rapidly eroded by the **dense wind** (1.75× Earth's
density), creating landscapes of:

* **Rounded Ridges:** Mountains with smooth profiles, devoid of sharp peaks.
* **Vast Plateaux:** Tablelands of light sedimentary and volcanic rock, uplifted by tectonics but levelled by erosion.
* **Broad Valleys:** Aeolian and fluvial erosion carves wide, shallow valleys.

## Mineral Resources

The planet's "light" chemistry and differentiation in a hydrated environment have distributed resources in a peculiar
manner.

### Abundant:

* **Light Metals:** Aluminium, Titanium, Magnesium and Lithium are omnipresent in the felsic crust.
* **Iron:** Moderately available in the crust (~10–13% by weight), concentrated in magnetite, haematite and siderite
  deposits. Less abundant than on Earth (~35% of Earth's crust), but sufficient for a developed iron industry. The
  richest deposits are found in volcanic formations (basalts), oxidised marine sediments and hydrothermal deposits.
* **Rare Earths (Lanthanides):** Found in easily accessible shallow veins, concentrated in granitic pegmatites and
  volcanic carbonatites.
* **Giant Crystals:** The high-pressure, high-temperature aqueous environment in the upper mantle is the perfect
  incubator for crystallisation. Cathedral-sized geodes, **quartz**, **beryl**, **topaz** and gemstones of colossal
  dimensions are common in volcanic zones and hydrothermal cavities.
* **Pure Silicon:** Ultra-high-purity quartz and silica sands, ideal for glass and optical technologies.
* **Salts and Evaporites:** Endorheic basins and salt lakes concentrate salts of potassium, lithium and boron.

### Rare and Precious:

* **Heavy Metals (Gold, Platinum, Uranium, Lead):** These dense metals sank toward the core during planetary formation
  due to the hydrated mantle's fluidity. Although globally rare at the surface, where they do outcrop they display
  exceptional purity from millennia of hydrothermal "washing". They are extracted mainly from:
    * **Ancient Meteorite Craters:** Impacts that brought in "alien" materials rich in siderophile metals.
    * **Mantle Outcrops:** Zones where tectonics has exposed deep mantle rocks (ophiolites).
    * **Deep Hydrothermal Deposits:** Oceanic springs that concentrate dissolved metals.

## Geological Cycle and Stability

### Carbon Cycle:

Volcanic CO₂ (2% of the atmosphere) is sequestered through:

* **Chemical Weathering:** Slow rain (0.8 g gravity) favours the dissolution of silicates, forming carbonates.
* **Oceanic Sedimentation:** Marine organisms fix carbon in calcareous shells.
* **Subduction:** Carbonates are dragged into the mantle and released again by volcanoes, closing the cycle in
  ~100 million years.

### Long-Term Stability:

* **Radiogenic Heat:** The mantle contains sufficient Uranium, Thorium and Potassium to sustain active convection for
  another 3–4 billion years.
* **Core Cooling:** The inner core grows slowly (~1 mm/year), fuelling the magnetic dynamo for at least 2 billion
  years.
* **Atmospheric Protection:** The robust magnetosphere (85% of Earth's field) guarantees protection of the dense
  atmosphere from stellar winds, even during Kepler's expansion phase (in ~1 billion years).

---

## Conclusion: A Living and Stable World

Ondgard is a geologically **young yet remarkably stable** planet. Its thin crust, lubricated hydrated mantle and
light-element-rich core create a dynamic system in continuous evolution, but without violence. Fragmented tectonics
release stress gradually through asynchronous creep, preventing catastrophic earthquakes. Diffuse, predominantly
effusive volcanoes shape the landscape with slow, predictable lava rivers. Rapid erosion caused by the dense wind
smooths the ridges, creating gentle, rounded landscapes. It is a world that **breathes** slowly, where geology is a
gradual and predictable process, not a sudden threat. Civilisations can prosper without fear of violent seismic or
volcanic catastrophes.
