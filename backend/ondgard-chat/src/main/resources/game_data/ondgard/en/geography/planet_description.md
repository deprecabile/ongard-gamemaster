# Description of the Planet Ondgard

## Star — Kepler

* **Name:** Kepler (Classification: K0IV — Orange Subgiant)
* **Type:** Orange Subgiant Star (Evolved)
* **Mass:** 1.08 M☉
* **Radius:** 1.72 R☉ (Subgiant Star)
* **Luminosity:** 1.36 L☉
* **Surface Temperature:** ~4,750 K
* **Appearance in the Sky:** Appears as a disc noticeably larger than Earth's Sun (apparent diameter ~0.74°), with an
  intense **orange-gold** colour. The light is warm, soft and low in ultraviolet (low sunburn risk).
* **Habitable Zone:** Ondgard orbits at 1.23 AU, receiving the same heat as Earth (90%), but with a luminous spectrum
  shifted toward red/infrared.

## Time System

## Satellites

The tidal system is dominated by a dynamic and complex interaction.

* **Base Tide (Astro):** The gravitational pull of the larger satellite provides the background rhythm — a steady cycle
  following its monthly orbit, though with lower intensity than Earth's tides.
* **Pulsating Tide (Shinosa):** The second body introduces chaos. Its frequent, close passages superimpose violent,
  sudden impulses ("rogue waves") onto the base cycle, breaking the regularity of the waters.
    * **Unpredictability:** The interference between the two cycles produces erratic water behaviour. The real danger
      for
      navigation lies not so much in maximum wave height as in the **speed** at which the tide rises and the treacherous
      cross-currents generated by the interaction of the two bodies.

### Astro

* **Type:** Rocky
* **Mean Radius:** 2,000 km
* **Mass:** 1.12 × 10²³ kg (roughly 1.5 times Earth's Moon)
* **Orbit:** Equatorial Circular
* **Orbital Parameters:** Perigee: **528,000 km** (Apogee: **528,000 km**) — distances from the planet's centre
* **Orbital Period:** Exactly 30 days (960 hours)
* **Atmosphere:** None
* **Appearance:** Given the greater distance, it appears in the sky slightly **smaller** than our Moon (about 85% of
  the apparent size), yet still very bright.

### Shinosa

* **Type:** Iron-Cobalt and Neodymium Core (Permanent Magnet)
* **Mean Radius:** ~60 km
* **Mass:** 7.2 × 10¹⁸ kg (roughly 1/10,000 of Earth's Moon)
* **Surface Magnetic Field:** ~1.3 Tesla (Extreme: about 26,000 times the standard terrestrial field)
* **Orbit:** Elliptical and Strongly Inclined (~60°)
* **Orbital Parameters:** Perigee: **25,000 km** (Apogee: **112,000 km**)
* **Orbital Period:** 45 hours
* **Atmosphere:** None
* **Effects:**
    * **Irregular Rhythm:** The satellite has a highly eccentric cycle. It spends most of its time far away and
      "silent", then plunges into a rapid, intense close passage.
    * **Moderate Tides:** Given the low mass, the gravitational tide is weak (a few centimetres). Water moves primarily
      through harmonic resonance and induced currents, creating anomalous flows but not destructive waves.
    * **Magnetic Inferno (Shield Reconnection):** The impact is not due to simple attraction but to a violent
      electrodynamic interaction. Shinosa behaves like a 1.3-Tesla "magnetic projectile" piercing the planet's
      magnetosphere.
        * **Reconnection Storms:** At perigee (25,000 km), the moon's field "tears" and reconnects the planetary field
          lines. This releases gigawatts of energy, injecting high-energy plasma directly into the ionosphere.
        * **Blackouts and Telluric Currents:** The plasma bombardment generates large electrical currents in the
          atmosphere, rendering compasses useless (the local field is drowned by the electromagnetic "noise" of the
          storm). Parasitic currents also form in the ground.
        * **Auroras and Lightning:** The sky lights up with auroras visible even during the day and "clear-sky
          lightning"
          caused by the potential difference between the overcharged ionosphere and the ground.

## Tidal System and Coastal Impact

The tidal system is dominated by Astro's regularity but complicated by Shinosa's eccentricity.
Compared to Earth, the overall intensity is **slightly lower**, yet the dynamics are far more chaotic and
unpredictable.

* **The Base Cycle (16.5 hours):** With a 32-hour planetary rotation and Astro's monthly orbit, two "Astro high tides"
  occur per day, roughly every **16.5 hours**.
* **Intensity and Numbers:**
    * **Base Tide (Astro):** The constant component, with a range of about **0.59 times** Earth's lunar tide.
    * **Shinosa's Peak:** Every **45 hours** (at perigee), Shinosa adds a gravitational impulse of **0.36 times**
      Earth's lunar tide.
    * **Combined Result:** Tides never reach Earth's maximum peaks (total **0.95** versus 1.46 for Earth's spring
      tides), but the interaction frequency creates a "nervous" sea with levels that shift in a non-cyclical manner.
  > [!NOTE]
  > **Technical Note: Wave Beat**
  > The real danger is not wave height but irregularity.
  > * **Earth:** Predictable sinusoidal cycle.
  > * **Ondgard:** Shinosa's impulse disrupts Astro's rhythm. **Sudden constructive interference** phenomena occur:
      > the sea level can remain stationary for hours and then rise in bursts when the two vectors align, confounding
      > those accustomed to regular tides.

* **Coastal Zones:** Cities are built on secure terraces not because of water height but because of the flow's
  unpredictability.
* **Inland Navigation:** The tidal current is less powerful than Earth's, but harbour entry windows for river ports are
  shorter and more irregular (16.5 h cycle versus 12.4 h).
