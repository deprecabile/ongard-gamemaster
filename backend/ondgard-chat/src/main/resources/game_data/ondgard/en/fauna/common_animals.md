# Common Fauna of Ondgard

Ondgard's fauna reflects the planet's physical conditions: reduced gravity (0.8g) and dense atmosphere (1.75 atm) favour gigantism and flight, while the high CO₂ (2%) has selected for enhanced respiratory systems and efficient blood buffers across all animal species. Animal sizes are on average larger than their terrestrial counterparts.

**Ondgard's biodiversity is staggering: an estimated 15–25 million animal species exist, a variety no catalogue could ever exhaust. Those listed here are merely a handful of particularly common or relevant species for daily life**, trade and adventure — an infinitesimal fraction of the planet's true faunal heritage. Countless other creatures populate every ecological niche, from the ocean depths to the canopies of the colossal forests.

### Familiar fauna

Alongside Ondgard's unique species, the planet hosts a wide variety of animals entirely analogous to those found on Earth. Wolves, bears, deer, wild boar, foxes, pigs, goats, reindeer, monkeys, eagles, hawks, snakes, frogs, freshwater and saltwater fish, insects of every kind — all the fauna one would expect in a world with temperate, boreal, tropical and marine biomes is present on Ondgard, with minor adaptations to reduced gravity and elevated CO₂ (slightly larger builds, more efficient lungs). These familiar animals make up the vast majority of fauna that inhabitants interact with on a daily basis. The species described in this document represent the **Ondgardian delta**: creatures unique to this world that exist on top of an otherwise recognisable faunal base, and that give Ondgard its distinctive character.

## Terrestrial Megafauna

### Saurofrondo
* **Type:** Migratory mega-herbivore
* **Size:** 9–12 m tall at the back, 20–30 tonnes
* **Appearance:** Massive sauropod-like body supported by four columnar legs. Long muscular neck ending in a small head with broad, flat jaws. Leathery slate-grey skin with dorsal bony plates. Stout tail used as a counterweight.
* **Habitat:** Grasslands, savannas and forest margins of the Gold Belt. Avoids dense forests where it cannot manoeuvre.
* **Behaviour:** Lives in migratory herds of 8–20 individuals following established seasonal routes. A passing herd creates wide, beaten trails that local populations use as natural roads. Docile unless provoked, but a defensive charge is lethal.
* **Ecological role:** Keystone herbivore that keeps corridors between forests open, preventing vegetation from closing passages. Its dung fertilises vast areas.
* **Notes:** Migrating herds are a well-known and predictable spectacle. Villages along the routes trade with the nomadic herders who follow them. Frequent encounter on grasslands, rare in forests.

### Cornoferro
* **Type:** Solitary armoured herbivore
* **Size:** 3–4 m at the withers, 6–10 tonnes
* **Appearance:** Stocky, powerful body resembling a cross between a rhinoceros and a triceratops. Massive head protected by a bony shield from which two main curved horns and a crest of smaller spikes protrude. Thick, rough skin in rusty-brown with metallic reflections caused by mineral deposits in the keratin.
* **Habitat:** Scrublands, forest clearings and hillsides. Prefers areas with partial cover.
* **Behaviour:** Solitary and territorial. Extremely aggressive when disturbed: charges without hesitation at anything perceived as a threat. Males in rut are especially dangerous. Marks territory by gouging furrows into the ground with its horns.
* **Ecological role:** Its habit of felling young trees and bushes creates clearings that promote understorey biodiversity.
* **Notes:** A real danger for travellers and caravans. Experienced hunters avoid it; the horns are prized but the risk is extreme. Infrequent encounter but potentially lethal.

### Varnok
* **Type:** Apex terrestrial predator
* **Size:** 4–5 m at the withers, 3–5 tonnes
* **Appearance:** Powerful biped resembling a large theropod. Enormous jaws lined with serrated teeth, with exposed gums of a vivid red. Dark skin with burgundy striations for camouflage among the red-purple vegetation. Short forelegs bearing curved claws. Long, rigid tail for balance.
* **Habitat:** Temperate forests and dense scrublands. Also hunts at grassland margins.
* **Behaviour:** Solitary ambush hunter. Stands motionless among the vegetation, exploiting its natural camouflage, then attacks with an explosive short-range charge. Territorial: a single Varnok dominates an area of dozens of square kilometres. Active primarily at twilight.
* **Ecological role:** Apex terrestrial predator. Controls mega-herbivore and mid-sized herbivore populations.
* **Notes:** The most feared terrestrial threat for travellers. Villages at forest margins maintain lookouts and night fires. Encountering one is rare but often fatal for the unprepared.

### Ombrafauce
* **Type:** Large feline quadruped predator
* **Size:** 180–220 cm at the withers, 800–1500 kg
* **Appearance:** Massive quadruped with a feline build, resembling a vastly enlarged sabre-toothed tiger. Powerful musculature visible beneath short, dense black-violet fur with darker striations, nearly invisible in the forest gloom. Broad head with powerful jaws and two curved upper canines 20–30 cm long. Wide paws with retractable claws. Yellow-amber eyes with excellent night vision.
* **Habitat:** Deep forests, wooded gorges and mountainous terrain with dense cover. Prefers environments where nocturnal camouflage is most effective.
* **Behaviour:** Solitary crepuscular-nocturnal predator. Hunts by silent stalking followed by an explosive short-range pounce, killing with a bite to the neck. Extremely territorial: marks vast areas with claw scratches on trees and scent marks. Males and females meet only for mating. Capable of climbing the giant trees despite its bulk.
* **Ecological role:** Apex terrestrial predator alongside the Varnok, but occupying different niches: the Varnok dominates open grasslands and scrublands, while the Ombrafauce reigns in the deep forests. Hunts Saltaroccia, Corvago and young Cornoferro.
* **Notes:** More stealthy and less conspicuous than the Varnok, but equally deadly. Loggers and forest hunters fear it more than the Varnok because it strikes without warning. Claw marks on trees (territorial signs) are the main warning: if the trees are scarred, you are in an Ombrafauce's territory. The dark pelt is extremely rare and of enormous value.

## Flying Megafauna

### Grandala
* **Type:** Giant raptor, apex aerial predator
* **Size:** 18–22 m wingspan, 200–300 kg
* **Appearance:** Massive bird with black-violet plumage and metallic blue reflections. Curved powerful beak; talons capable of lifting prey over 100 kg. Large golden eyes with exceptional sight.
* **Habitat:** Nests on the tallest treetops or inaccessible cliffs. Hunts over grasslands, coasts and forest clearings.
* **Behaviour:** Lives in monogamous pairs that patrol an enormous territory. Hunts by circling at high altitude, then diving at devastating speed. The pair coordinates attacks. Nests once a year with 1–2 eggs.
* **Ecological role:** Apex aerial predator. Hunts Saltaroccia, Vispola, Corvago and occasionally isolated young Saurofrondo.
* **Notes:** The shadow of a Grandala in flight is an instinctive warning for every ground creature. Nests serve as landmarks for navigators. Visual encounter frequent (they soar at high altitude); direct attack on humanoids very rare.

### Velarca
* **Type:** Colonial piscivorous draconiform
* **Size:** 12–16 m wingspan, 100–180 kg
* **Appearance:** Flying reptile with wing membranes supported by elongated digits, resembling a large pterosaur. Sleek aerodynamic body; long narrow snout with thin rear-pointing teeth to hold fish. Blue-grey dorsal colouration, silvery belly.
* **Habitat:** Coasts, estuaries, islands and large lakes. Nests in noisy colonies on coastal cliffs.
* **Behaviour:** Colonial: colonies number 30–100 individuals. Fishes by diving from height, catching fish with its snout. Collective hunting flights at sunset are a common sight in coastal ports. Seasonal migrations along the coasts.
* **Ecological role:** Specialised piscivore. Links marine and coastal ecosystems by transporting nutrients (guano) onto the cliffs.
* **Notes:** Velarca colonies indicate fish-rich waters. Fishermen follow them to find schools of Argentone. Guano is collected as fertiliser. Very frequent encounter on coasts, harmless to humanoids.

### Torvesca
* **Type:** Large scavenger vulture
* **Size:** 10–14 m wingspan, 80–150 kg
* **Appearance:** Dull black plumage with a collar of greyish-white feathers around the bare, wrinkled neck. Bald reddish head with a powerful beak suited for tearing tough carcasses. Broad, squared wings optimised for prolonged soaring.
* **Habitat:** Wherever megafauna exists. Frequent above grasslands, migration routes and battlefields.
* **Behaviour:** Obligate scavenger. Soars for hours on thermals, scanning the ground for carcasses. Gathers in groups of 5–20 around a Saurofrondo or Cornoferro carcass, feasting for days. Popularly called "the gravedigger of the sky".
* **Ecological role:** Essential for biomass recycling. Without Torvesca, megafauna carcasses would cause epidemics.
* **Notes:** A circle of Torvesca in the sky signals a fresh carcass — useful for explorers (food) or dangerous (the predator may still be nearby). Culturally associated with death but respected for their role.

## Medium Terrestrial Fauna

### Lurcano
* **Type:** Pack predator
* **Size:** 110–130 cm at the withers, 180–280 kg
* **Appearance:** Imposing canid of tiger-like proportions, combining muscular power with surprising agility for its bulk. Massive build with a deep chest and broad shoulders, yet tucked flanks and long legs that grant it an explosive sprint. Elongated, lupine muzzle with powerful jaws; erect, pointed ears. Dense grey-brown fur with darker shading along the back and shoulders. Long, bushy tail, carried low at rest and level during a run.
* **Behaviour:** Hunts in coordinated packs of 6–15 using encirclement and exhaustion tactics. Rigid social hierarchy led by an alpha pair. Communicates with guttural howls audible over great distances. Active mainly at twilight and night.
* **Habitat:** Grasslands, open scrublands and forest margins. Adapts to nearly any non-desert environment.
* **Ecological role:** Mid-level predator. Hunts Saltaroccia, Corvago, Vispola and unguarded livestock. Prey of the Varnok.
* **Notes:** A serious threat to lone travellers and flocks. Packs avoid armed groups but can become bold in winter when prey is scarce. The fur is used for sturdy clothing. The description above refers to the most common variant, found in temperate forests. However, numerous regional variants exist that differ in coat colour and may vary slightly in size: steppe Lurcano are leaner with a tawny-sand coat, boreal-forest Lurcano are bulkier with thick silvery-grey fur, while those in southern scrublands sport a short reddish-brown coat.

### Saltaroccia
* **Type:** Wild herbivore, primary game animal
* **Size:** 130–160 cm at the withers, 120–200 kg
* **Appearance:** Resembles a sturdy deer with powerful legs suited to rough terrain. Broad branching antlers in males, absent in females. Thick coat in bronze-russet tones, turning dark grey in winter. Wide, grippy hooves.
* **Habitat:** Rocky hills, mountain slopes, temperate forests and clearings. Named for its agility on rough ground.
* **Behaviour:** Lives in herds of 10–30 led by an elderly female. Males are solitary outside the mating season. Selective browser feeding on shoots, bark and low foliage. Fast and agile runner, capable of impressive leaps among the rocks.
* **Ecological role:** Mid-sized herbivore; primary game for predators (Lurcano, Varnok, Grandala) and humanoid hunters.
* **Notes:** The most important game animal of Ondgard. Its meat is the staple protein in rural areas. Antlers and hides are traded. A Saltaroccia hunt is a common activity for villages and nobles alike.

### Corvago
* **Type:** Understorey omnivore
* **Size:** 70–90 cm at the withers, 80–150 kg
* **Appearance:** Stocky, compact body resembling a large wild boar. Broad muzzle with protruding curved tusks. Bristly dark-brown-to-black hair; thick skin. Short, sturdy legs with hard hooves.
* **Habitat:** Temperate forests, understorey, scrublands and field margins. Frequent wherever there is plant cover.
* **Behaviour:** Opportunistic omnivore: digs up roots, tubers, fungi and larvae with its powerful snout, literally ploughing the forest floor. Small family groups of 3–8. Aggressive when cornered, especially mothers with young.
* **Ecological role:** Ecosystem engineer. Constant rooting turns the soil, promotes germination and mixes forest litter. Prey of Lurcano and Varnok.
* **Notes:** Abundant and flavourful game. Can be a pest for farmers (destroys gardens and crops). Common and relatively safe hunt. Smoked Corvago meat is a staple food.

## Small Fauna

### Vispola
* **Type:** Prey herbivore, prolific
* **Size:** 35–50 cm, 2–4 kg
* **Appearance:** Resembles a sturdy hare with shorter, rounded ears. Dense sandy-russet fur with a pale belly. Long powerful hind legs for rapid sprints. Large lateral eyes for panoramic vision.
* **Habitat:** Grasslands, clearings, forest margins, cultivated areas. Present wherever there is grass.
* **Behaviour:** Herbivore grazing on grass and shoots. Extremely prolific (4–6 litters per year, 3–5 young each). Lives in colonies with shallow burrows. First instinct: zigzag flight.
* **Ecological role:** Universal prey. Foundation of the food chain for Lurcano, Rossiccio, Grandala, Stridala and virtually every small-to-medium predator.
* **Notes:** Common and accessible meat, hunted with traps and slings. Colonies can damage crops. Present in any temperate setting — if a place seems devoid of fauna, there is probably still a Vispola hiding.

### Ghironda
* **Type:** Nocturnal burrowing omnivore
* **Size:** 40–60 cm, 5–10 kg
* **Appearance:** Robust, low-slung body resembling a badger. Dense silvery-grey fur with a black dorsal stripe. Pointed snout with a moist, sensitive nose. Short, broad legs with powerful digging claws. Short furry tail.
* **Habitat:** Forests, hills and soft ground. Digs complex, deep burrow systems (known as "ghirondaie").
* **Behaviour:** Nocturnal and reclusive. Feeds on roots, tubers, larvae, small reptiles and eggs. Burrows may be occupied for generations, becoming extensive underground labyrinths. Solitary or in small family units. Hard to spot by day.
* **Ecological role:** Burrowing aerates the soil and creates shelters used by many other species. Controls invertebrate populations.
* **Notes:** The fur is prized for its softness. Abandoned ghirondaie serve as emergency refuges for small animals and occasionally for desperate travellers (entrances can be 50–60 cm wide). Edible but unremarkable meat.

### Rossiccio
* **Type:** Small opportunistic predator
* **Size:** 50–70 cm (plus 30–40 cm tail), 4–8 kg
* **Appearance:** Resembles a slender fox with a sharp muzzle and pointed triangular ears. Dense, soft fur of an intense copper-red (hence the name), with darker tail tip and legs. Watchful amber eyes.
* **Habitat:** Any environment with cover: forests, hedgerows, cultivated land, settlement outskirts. Very adaptable.
* **Behaviour:** Solitary nocturnal hunter. Preys on Vispola, small rodents, ground-nesting birds and eggs. Opportunist: raids henhouses and food stores if unprotected. Intelligent and wary, hard to catch.
* **Ecological role:** Small predator controlling Vispola and rodent populations. Prey of Lurcano and Grandala.
* **Notes:** The copper-red pelt is highly prized for clothing and trimmings, and is a valued trade good. Hunted with traps. Culturally associated with cunning in many Ondgardian traditions.

## Birds and Minor Flyers

### Pennacremisi
* **Type:** Ground-dwelling bird, prized game
* **Size:** 50–70 cm, 2–4 kg
* **Appearance:** Resembles a large pheasant. The male sports brilliant crimson plumage with golden reflections on a long fan-shaped tail used in courtship displays. The female is plainer, with brown tones and reddish striations for camouflage.
* **Habitat:** Understorey of temperate forests, clearing margins, hedgerows and low scrub.
* **Behaviour:** Ground-dweller: runs more than it flies, bursting into explosive flight only when startled. Feeds on seeds, berries, insects and small invertebrates. Males are territorial and their call — a sharp, melodious trill — is a characteristic sound of Ondgardian forests.
* **Ecological role:** Seed disperser. Prey of Rossiccio, Lurcano and raptors.
* **Notes:** Prized game served at noble tables. Hunting Pennacremisi with a bow is considered a refined sport. The crimson feathers are used as ornaments and have modest trade value.

### Stridala
* **Type:** Omnivorous bird, urban opportunist
* **Size:** 50–65 cm, 1–2 kg
* **Appearance:** Resembles a large crow. Glossy black plumage with violet iridescence. Sturdy versatile beak; intelligent black eyes. Broad wings for its size; agile flight.
* **Habitat:** Wherever humanoid settlements exist. Also in forests, coasts and grasslands, but highest concentration in villages and cities.
* **Behaviour:** Omnivorous opportunist: refuse, carrion, seeds, insects, small animals, fruit. Remarkable intelligence: uses tools, solves problems, remembers faces. Lives in noisy flocks. Its call — a harsh, insistent screech — is the background sound of every settlement.
* **Ecological role:** Urban and rural scavenger. Seed disperser. Indicator of humanoid presence.
* **Notes:** Ubiquitous and impossible to eradicate. Considered a nuisance but useful (cleans up refuse). Some cultures regard it as a bearer of omens. The absence of Stridala in a settlement is an unsettling sign.

### Dardavento
* **Type:** Giant flying insect, insect predator
* **Size:** 80–110 cm wingspan, 0.5–1 kg
* **Appearance:** Giant dragonfly with four transparent iridescent wings producing a low hum. Elongated segmented body; colouration varies by subspecies: electric blue, emerald green or amber. Enormous compound eyes covering most of the head.
* **Habitat:** Wetlands, riverbanks and lakeshores, marshes, forests near watercourses.
* **Behaviour:** Aerial predator of smaller insects, catching them in flight with its forelegs. Hovers and makes sudden dashes. Active during the central hours of the day. Aquatic larvae spend 2–3 years in watercourses before metamorphosis.
* **Ecological role:** Controls insect populations. Clean-water indicator: where Dardavento are found, the water is drinkable.
* **Notes:** Harmless to humanoids. Dardavento presence is a positive sign for explorers seeking water. The iridescent wings are occasionally collected as decorative curiosities.

## Aquatic Fauna

### Argentone
* **Type:** Large migratory fish, commercial resource
* **Size:** 1–2 m, 30–80 kg
* **Appearance:** Resembles an oversized salmon. Streamlined, powerful body covered in brilliant silvery scales (hence the name). Dark blue-green back, white-silver belly. Sturdy jaws with small teeth.
* **Habitat:** Coastal oceans and rivers. Seasonal migrations: ascends rivers to spawn, returns to sea to feed.
* **Behaviour:** Spectacular migrations in shoals of thousands ascending rivers against the current, leaping powerfully over rapids and waterfalls. The run season is an eagerly awaited event along all major rivers.
* **Ecological role:** Transports marine nutrients inland. Prey of Velarca, Crestonda and countless river predators. Post-spawning carcasses fertilise riparian forests.
* **Notes:** A fundamental economic resource for coastal and riverine communities. Traded fresh, smoked and salt-cured. The run season determines fishing calendars and trade fairs in many regions.

### Crestonda
* **Type:** Coastal marine reptile, group predator
* **Size:** 4–7 m long, 300–800 kg
* **Appearance:** Hydrodynamic marine reptile resembling a small ichthyosaur. Elongated snout with conical teeth; bony dorsal crest running from head to mid-back (hence the name). Smooth blue-grey skin with a pale belly. Broad flippers and a crescent tail.
* **Habitat:** Coastal waters, bays and estuaries. Rarely in the open sea.
* **Behaviour:** Lives in pods of 3–8 that hunt cooperatively, encircling fish shoals and driving them to the surface. Breathes air: surfaces regularly, visible as a series of crests cutting through the water.
* **Ecological role:** Mid-level coastal predator. Controls fish populations and competes with fishermen for the same resources.
* **Notes:** Generally not aggressive towards boats, but can damage fishing nets and steal catches. Territorial pods near ports are a well-known complication for fishermen. The hide is used for waterproof belts and boots.

### Nerobianco
* **Type:** Large solitary marine mammal predator
* **Size:** 10–15 m long, 8–15 tonnes
* **Appearance:** Massive marine mammal with a hydrodynamic body, resembling a cross between an orca and a sperm whale. Glossy black dorsal colouration shifting sharply to a crisp ivory-white belly (hence the name). Large, squared head with enormous jaws bearing thick conical teeth. Tall falcate dorsal fin. Broad pectoral fins. Single blowhole atop the skull.
* **Habitat:** Deep coastal waters, straits, fjords and open seas. Approaches the coast in winter following Argentone migrations.
* **Behaviour:** Solitary, territorial predator. Hunts large fish, Crestonda and other marine reptiles with deep dives and attacks from below. Surfaces to breathe with a powerful spout visible from afar. Males are fiercely territorial and attack any rival entering their waters. Females are less aggressive and tolerate their young for 2–3 years.
* **Ecological role:** Apex marine predator. Controls Crestonda and large fish populations. Its presence indicates deep, life-rich waters.
* **Notes:** Feared by sailors: a territorial Nerobianco may attack small boats it perceives as intruders. Coastal shipping routes take known territories into account. The blubber is used as lamp fuel and the teeth as carving material, but hunting is extremely dangerous and practised only by specialised coastal communities.

## Domestic Animals

### Horse
* **Type:** Domestic equine for transport, hauling and war
* **Size:** Variable by breed (140–170 cm at the withers)
* **Appearance:** Analogous to the terrestrial horse, with different breeds selected for specific purposes. Ondgardian breeds tend towards slightly sturdier legs and larger lungs as an adaptation to the high CO₂.
* **Habitat:** Domestic. Bred across all of Ondgard, from the steppes of Thia to the stables of Voth.
* **Behaviour:** Same as terrestrial horses: herd animal, trainable, bonds with its rider.
* **Ecological role:** Domestic. Some feral populations on the eastern steppes.
* **Notes:** Primary means of transport. Thia war breeds are the most prized. The horse remains a valuable asset, out of reach for many peasants.

### Bovargo
* **Type:** Domestic bovine for labour and production
* **Size:** 150–180 cm at the withers, 700–1200 kg
* **Appearance:** Massive, patient bovine larger than terrestrial oxen. Broad, robust build; short curved horns. Thick, woolly coat, generally dark brown or black. Wide muzzle, placid eyes.
* **Habitat:** Domestic. Bred in all agricultural areas, especially the plains and valleys of the Gold Belt.
* **Behaviour:** Docile and hardy. Used for ploughing, heavy hauling and goods transport as well as milk, meat and leather production. Grazing herds are a common sight in the countryside.
* **Ecological role:** Domestic. Grazing shapes the rural landscape.
* **Notes:** Pillar of the agricultural economy. Bovargo milk is the base for cheeses and dairy products. The leather is a fundamental raw material for artisans. Slower than the horse but stronger and more enduring for heavy work.

### Lanaro
* **Type:** Small domestic ruminant for wool, milk and meat
* **Size:** 60–80 cm at the withers, 40–70 kg
* **Appearance:** Resembles a cross between a sheep and a goat. Compact body with thick, wavy fleece ranging from cream-white to grey and brown. Short muzzle with small curved horns in both sexes. Agile, sure-footed legs on rough terrain.
* **Habitat:** Domestic. Bred everywhere from hills to steppes. Particularly important on the Thia steppes where it is the pillar of the nomadic economy.
* **Behaviour:** Gregarious, docile, easy to raise. Browses grass, shrubs and low foliage. Adapts to poor pastures where the Bovargo cannot survive. Flocks of 30–200 are common.
* **Ecological role:** Domestic. Grazing on the steppes keeps the grasslands open.
* **Notes:** Lanaro wool is the basic clothing fabric across all of Ondgard. The milk is used for aged cheeses typical of nomadic cultures. For the clans of Thia, flock size is a direct measure of wealth and status. An affordable animal even for poor families.
