# Religion of Ondgard: Archeism (The Cult of the Eleven)

## Introduction

**Archeism** is the dominant religion on Ondgard, practised by the vast majority of sentient races (Humans, Elves,
Dwarves, and in different forms by the others as well). Unlike many faiths based on abstract deities or natural forces,
Archeism is founded on a **real, documented historical event**, albeit distorted by millennia of oral tradition,
mythologisation and misunderstanding.

At the heart of the faith is the veneration of the **Eleven Travellers** (or "Siderals"), beings who came from the
stars approximately 10,000 years ago, walked on Ondgard for two centuries and then ascended to the sky once more.

---

## The Founding Myth: The Historical Genesis

### 1. The Age of Darkness (−10,000 Y.Z.)

Before the arrival of the Eleven, Ondgard's races lived in a state of tribal barbarity. No kingdoms, writing or
advanced agriculture existed. Shamanism, animism and blood cults fragmented the peoples into eternal micro-conflicts.
Humans, Elves and Orcs were little more than scattered hunter-gatherers.

### 2. The Descent of the Siderals

Approximately ten thousand years ago, the sky opened. Legend tells that **eleven stars** detached from the firmament
and settled upon the earth. They were not incorporeal spirits but beings of flesh and matter, though of a substance
unlike anything seen on Ondgard.

* **Appearance:** They were described as extremely tall, with skin that reflected light like polished obsidian or metal,
  faceless or hidden behind immovable masks.
* **Nature:** They did not age, did not eat food known to mortals, and possessed tools capable of shaping matter
  (interpreted as "sceptres of power" or divine magic).

### 3. The Century of the Walking

For roughly 200 years, the Eleven travelled across the entire globe.

* **The First (The Architectus):** One of them always walked ahead. He was the one who chose the path.
* **The Ten (The Custodians):** The other ten followed, carrying out incomprehensible tasks: collecting soil samples,
  observing storms, cataloguing beasts.

The primitive tribes followed at a distance. Seeing them immune to disease, wild beasts and weather, they began to
worship them. The Eleven never sought adoration, but tolerated the natives' presence, benignly ignoring them as a
person ignores ants that do not obstruct them. This **indifference** was interpreted theologically as "Silent
Judgement" or "Pure Observation".

Their physical presence on every continent (thanks to rapid transport technologies interpreted as teleportation or
divine flight) ensured that **all cultures** on the planet encountered them. This is why Archeism is universal: it is
not the vision of a single local prophet but the shared experience of an entire planet.

---

## Year Zero: The Revelation (The Pact)

Although the Eleven rarely spoke (and in a harsh, metallic language among themselves), history is divided into "Before"
and "After" a single specific event.

**The Event:**
On the slopes of a sacred mountain (today a pilgrimage destination), the **First** halted. For the first time, he
addressed a native directly (according to legend, an elderly wise human or elf, depending on the tradition).
The Sideral gave no orders of conquest or dominion. Instead, he delivered an object — or perhaps articulated a series
of concepts — that were painstakingly transcribed in the earliest forms of proto-writing.

* **Historical Interpretation:** Probably an attempt at basic education or a cultural bequest before departure.
* **Religious Interpretation:** The delivery of the **Universal Law**, an ethical and civic code to elevate mortals
  from barbarity.

From that day (Year 0), the Eleven began their ascent. One by one, they left the planet (or died/disintegrated,
according to heretical apocrypha), leaving the peoples orphaned but "enlightened".

### The Dating System: Y.Z. (Year Zero)

The Revelation event on the sacred mountain marks the **zero point** of Ondgard's universal calendar. All races and
cultures, regardless of their local traditions, use this dating system as the official reference:

* **B.Z. (Before Zero):** Events preceding the Revelation.
    * **Year −200 B.Z.** approx.: Arrival of the Eleven on the planet (beginning of the "Century of the Walking")
    * **Year −1 B.Z.**: The last year before the Revelation
* **Y.Z. (Year Zero):** The year of the Revelation, when Othmar delivered the Universal Law.
* **After Y.Z.:** All subsequent years are counted progressively.
    * **Year +200 Y.Z.** approx.: Ascension of the last of the Eleven
    * **Year 5,000 Y.Z.**: Discovery of the Plates of Knowledge (beginning of organised magic)
    * **Year 6,247 Y.Z.**: Promulgation of the Decree of the Sacred Body
    * **Year ~10,000 Y.Z.**: Current era

This dating system is one of the pillars of Ondgard's cultural unity: even kingdoms at war, different races and
divergent religious sects recognise Year Zero as the shared temporal foundation.

## Theology and Structure

### The Pantheon: The One and the Ten

The religion is **Hierarchical Henotheism**. The Eleven are venerated as deities, but with distinct roles.

1. **Othmar, The Architectus (The First):**
    * God of Civilisation, Time and Destiny.
    * Depicted as an imposing figure with a single-mirror mask.
    * He is the one who guides.

2. **The Ten (The Aspects):**
   Each "Disciple" was associated by the tribes with specific spheres of influence, based (often erroneously) on the
   actions they performed during their stay.
    * *Kael (The Warrior/Protector):* Perhaps the security officer, seen repelling predators with weapons of light.
      God of Just War and Defence.
    * *Sybilla (The Sage):* She who analysed plants. Goddess of Knowledge, Magic and Medicine.
    * *Ghor (The Builder):* He who collected rock samples. God of Craftsmanship, Earth and the Dwarves.
    * *Vex (The Observer):* He who watched the stars. God of Navigation and Travel.
    * *Liora (The Healer):* She who tended wounds. Goddess of Medicine and Compassion.
    * *Morthos (The Judge):* He who punished the wicked. God of Justice and Punishment.
    * *Ebfisium (The Smith):* He who forged metals. God of Fire and the Forge.
    * *Vesper (The Silent):* He who never spoke. God of Silence and Meditation.
    * *Nym (The Mother):* She who gave life. Goddess of Life and Fertility.
    * *Dyara (The Huntress):* She who was seen in the jungle felling enormous beasts with a single blow. Goddess of
      the Hunt and Survival.

### The Sacred Book: "The Chronicles of the Walking"

It is not a dictated text but a collection of chronicles. "The Chronicles of the Walking" narrate the movements of the
Eleven, their (rare) gestures and the interpretations of the first priests.

* Every verse is the subject of millennial debate: *"Why did Othmar halt by the river for three days? Did he mean to
  teach us patience, or was he analysing the water?"*

### Geographical Variations (Not Racial)

Since Ondgard's societies have been mixed for millennia (cosmopolitan kingdoms where Humans, Dwarves and Elves
coexist), the religion is **homogeneous**. There is no "God of the Elves" or "God of the Dwarves". Differences are
regional:

* **Northern Kingdoms (Mountain areas):** Tend to emphasise the figure of *Ghor (The Builder)* and physical
  resilience, regardless of whether the worshipper is a dwarf or a human.
* **Coastal Cities:** Venerate *Vex (The Observer)* more for protection at sea.

## 10,000 Years Later: The Current Situation

After ten millennia, historical truth is almost impossible to distinguish from myth.

1. **Orthodoxy and Distortion:** No one any longer knows they were "aliens" or explorers. For everyone they are
   **Gods**. Suggesting they were mortals or technologists is the supreme heresy ("Materialism").
2. **The Relics:** Extremely rare objects left by the Eleven exist (fragments of indestructible metal, sealed
   containers). They are the most sacred relics in the world, guarded in the major temples. They might be simple
   technological refuse (an empty container, a broken tool), but to the faithful they are the chalice of life or the
   sword of judgement.
3. **The Waiting:** The central doctrine is that the Eleven will return to judge whether the peoples have followed the
   "Path of Civilisation" traced at Year Zero.

Archeism provides the social and political structure that unites different kingdoms: a king rules because he is
"placed upon the throne by the shadow of Othmar".

## The Relationship Between Religion and Magic: The Great Contradiction

The relationship between Archeism and magical practice is one of the most complex theological, political and social
knots on Ondgard. It is a story of **double truth**, popular misunderstanding and exploitation of power.

### The Historical Truth

Magic on Ondgard is not a divine gift but a **discipline** based on the manipulation of Dimensional Rifts. The first
mages in history did not discover this knowledge through mystical illumination but through **artefacts left by the
Eleven**.

**The Plates of Knowledge:**
Approximately 5,000 years ago, in various locations across the planet (often near ancient Sideral encampments),
**metallic plates** inscribed with diagrams, symbols and geometric sequences were found. They were not texts in the
traditional sense but **technical schematics**: representations of energy flows, conversion geometries and maps of
phenomena now known as Dimensional Rifts.

* **Incomprehensible Script:** The symbols matched no language on Ondgard. It took centuries of study to decipher even
  the basic concepts.
* **Practical Nature:** These were not philosophical or religious teachings but **operating manuals**. The Eleven
  probably used them to catalogue the planet's energy phenomena during their exploration.
* **The First Order:** The earliest scholars who managed to interpret the plates founded what would become the
  **Order of the Transducers** (or simply "The Order"), the first organised school of magic.

**The Spread of Knowledge:**
The very first scholars who deciphered the plates initially acted in secrecy, forming a small, reserved sect. However,
over the following centuries knowledge inevitably spread. Today it is common knowledge among mages and scholars that
**magic derives from the Plates of Knowledge**, artefacts found in various locations across the planet.

What remains debated is the **origin** of these plates:

* **Ancient Civilisations Theory:** Some hold that the plates belonged to lost civilisations of Ondgard, predating the
  arrival of the Eleven.
* **Divine Gift Theory:** Others (erroneously) believe they were a deliberate gift from Othmar, intentionally left to
  elevate mortals.
* **Sideral Artefacts Theory:** A minority of scholars hypothesise they were tools or technical documentation left by
  the Eleven, but this interpretation is controversial because it would imply a non-divine nature of the Siderals.

Most mages today study the plates pragmatically, without excessive concern for their origin. There is no official
position from the Order: each person holds their own interpretation.

---

### The Popular Misunderstanding: Shinosa, the "Cursed Moon"

For common folk, magic is inseparably linked to **Shinosa**, the magnetic moon.

**Why the Misunderstanding?**

1. **Visible Phenomena:** When Shinosa passes at perigee, the sky blazes with multicoloured auroras, clear-sky
   lightning rips through the air and compasses go haywire. These phenomena are **spectacular and terrifying**.
2. **Apparent Coincidence:** Dimensional Rifts (the dimensional fractures from which mages draw energy) are chaotic
   and unpredictable, but tend to appear more frequently during electromagnetic storms. When Shinosa unleashes its
   storms, Rifts multiply. Mages are therefore more active during these events.
3. **False Causal Association:** The people see: *Shinosa passes → Lightning and auroras → Mages cast spells*.
   Logical (but wrong) conclusion: **"Mages draw power from Shinosa"**.

**The Dark Legend:**
In popular culture, Shinosa has become **"The Moon of Chaos"** or **"The Broken Eye"**. It is believed to be an
imprisoned celestial creature, a fragment of a fallen Eleventh Sideral, or even a hostile entity that the Eleven
failed to defeat before ascending.

---

### The Ecclesiastical Doctrine: The Condemnation of Magic

Orthodox Archeism, led by the **Curia of the Interpreters** (the supreme ecclesiastical hierarchy), has developed a
clear and inflexible theological position on magic.

#### The "Decree of the Sacred Body" (Year 6,247 Y.Z.)

This edict, promulgated during the **Iron Council** (an assembly of the highest patriarchs), established the official
doctrine:

> *"The body is a gift of the Eleven, temple of mortal life. Those who corrupt it with the fire of the sky (Shinosa)
> betray the Pact. Magic is hubris, a defiance of the elements, and brings only destruction."*

**Key Points of the Doctrine:**

1. **Sanctity of the Body:** The Eleven taught (according to the interpretation of *The Chronicles of the Walking*)
   the value of life and physical preservation. To mutilate or burn one's own body is sin.
2. **Backlash as Divine Punishment:** The random burns that strike mages when they fail a spell are seen as **direct
   judgement from Othmar**. "Those who play with celestial fire burn by divine will."
3. **Shinosa as Temptation:** The magnetic moon is interpreted as a **trial** left by the Eleven. Resisting the
   temptation to use its power is a sign of faith; yielding is sin.

**Practical Consequences:**

* **Excommunication:** Practising mages are formally excluded from the sacraments (blessed marriages, sacred burials,
  access to the major temples).
* **Social Stigma:** In deeply religious rural communities, mages are feared and despised. They are called
  *"Touched by Shinosa"* or *"The Burned"*.
* **No Active Persecution (Officially):** The Church does not order pogroms or burnings (unlike true heresies), but
  encourages social marginalisation.

---

### The Great Rupture: The Political Origin of the Condemnation

Between Year 5,000 and 6,200 Y.Z., magic underwent a dramatic evolution that led to the present-day ban.

#### 1. The Carbonari Phase (5,000–6,000 Y.Z.)

For a millennium after the discovery of the Plates, knowledge remained secret, guarded by very small circles of
initiates.

* **Zero Impact:** Mages were extremely few, cautious and invisible to society.
* **Controlled Mortality:** Being few and carefully selected, accidents were rare and unremarkable.

#### 2. The Great Dissemination (6,000–6,100 Y.Z.)

Around year 6,000, a group of rebel transducers began distributing simplified (and often inaccurate) copies of the
Plates' diagrams.

* Magic became "fashionable" among the bored nobility and then, gradually, filtered down to the common people as a
  promise of easy power.
* Spontaneous cults and unofficial schools sprang up.

#### 3. The Century of Blood (6,100–6,200 Y.Z.)

The uncontrolled spread led to demographic catastrophe. Without the iron discipline of the ancient orders, self-taught
practitioners died in their thousands.

* **Explosive Youth Mortality:** The mortality rate among improvised apprentices reached 80–90%.
* **Social Crisis:** Entire villages lost a generation of young people, burned alive trying to ignite a fire or move
  a stone with their minds. The countryside depopulated, the agricultural economy collapsed.

**But rulers also saw the other side of the coin:**

* The few who survived became **living weapons**. A single experienced mage could set an enemy fleet ablaze or
  demolish fortified walls.
* Magic was clearly a **strategic art**. A kingdom without mages would be vulnerable against enemies who possessed
  them.

#### The Cynical Solution: Instrumentalising Religion

The rulers understood they needed to **control access to magic** without eliminating it entirely. The solution was
brilliant and ruthless:

**They actively pushed the Curia of the Interpreters to condemn magic as heresy.**

1. **The Secret Pact (circa Year 6,200 Y.Z.):**
    * The most powerful kings secretly summoned the Church's patriarchs.
    * They presented demographic data: "Magic is killing our people. It is a plague that must be stopped."
    * They offered political and financial support in exchange for a religious edict against magical practice.

2. **The Decree of the Sacred Body (Year 6,247 Y.Z.):**
    * The Church officially promulgated the condemnation, but with an apparently sincere theological justification:
      "The body is a gift of the Eleven; to burn it is sin."
    * The decree never mentioned the political pressure. It appeared to be a purely spiritual decision.

3. **The Rural Propaganda:**
    * Priests were sent to the countryside to preach against magic.
    * The narrative of Shinosa as "Cursed Moon" and of mages as "Touched by Evil" spread.
    * Peasant families, already terrified by the deaths of their children, embraced the doctrine with relief: "It is
      sin, therefore our youngsters must not attempt it."

#### The Lost Truth

At the time of the Secret Pact (6,200 Y.Z.), the higher ecclesiastical hierarchies **knew** the condemnation was a
political move and that the Plates were not demonic.
However, after 4,000 years, this truth has been lost even within the Church.

* **Dogma:** Today, bishops and priests sincerely believe in the wickedness of magic.
* **The Pontiff's Secret:** It is whispered that only the **Supreme Custodian**, upon election, is granted access to
  the Black Archive, where the true history of the Plates and the Pact with the Kings is kept. He alone bears the
  burden of absolute truth.

#### The Hypocritical System: Public Condemnation, Private Use

Once the ecclesiastical condemnation was secured, the rulers built a system of **institutionalised double truth**:

1. **In the Countryside: Total Repression**
    * Religious stigma discouraged young peasants from practising magic.
    * The agricultural workforce was preserved.
    * Deeply religious rural communities marginalised the few who attempted it anyway.

2. **In the Capitals: Secret Academies**
    * Kings secretly funded **elite schools of magic**, accessible only to nobles or state-selected candidates.
    * These institutions had resources, expert masters and safer methods (mortality rate reduced to 50–60%).
    * Trained mages became **weapons of state**, bound by oaths of fealty.

3. **The Scapegoat:**
    * If a mage caused collateral damage (fires, civilian deaths), they were publicly sacrificed to the mob to appease
      the Church.
    * This maintained the illusion that the kings respected the religious doctrine.

4. **Numerical Control:**
    * The system ensured mages remained a **restricted, controllable class**.
    * Numerous enough to be useful in war, but too few to become an independent or revolutionary political force.

#### The Order's Position

The Order of the Transducers finds itself in a delicate position:

* **They cannot oppose the rulers** because they are the only ones who fund research.
* **They cannot openly oppose the Church**, or they would be annihilated.
* **They must accept marginalisation**, despite being the most advanced scholars in understanding the Plates.
* **Internal Debate:** Within the Order, diverse interpretations of the Plates' origin coexist, but the majority tends
  to believe they are artefacts left by the Eleven, which would make the Order the sole true heirs of Sideral
  knowledge.

---

### The Three Truths of Ondgard

In the end, **three narratives** about magic coexist, each believed by different groups:

| Group          | Believed Truth                                                                                                                                  | Motivation                                                                             |
|----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **The People** | "Magic comes from Shinosa, the cursed moon. It is sin and brings ruin."                                                                         | Misunderstanding based on visible coincidences + ecclesiastical indoctrination.        |
| **The Church** | "Magic is hubris, a defiance of the Pact of the Eleven. It must be discouraged to preserve life."                                               | Sincere theological interpretation (for some) + tool of social control (for others).   |
| **The Mages**  | "Magic derives from the Plates of Knowledge. The origin of the Plates is debated: ancient civilisations, gift of Othmar, or Sideral artefacts." | Knowledge widespread among scholars, but without consensus on interpreting the origin. |

---

### Narrative Implications

This conflict creates richly layered tensions:

* **Tormented Mages:** A devout mage lives in anguish — using knowledge that their faith condemns.
* **Political Hypocrisy:** A king who prays in public and employs mages in secret is vulnerable to blackmail.
* **Selective Witch Hunts:** When a scapegoat is needed (famine, pestilence), mages are blamed.
* **Theological Debate:** Scholars and theologians argue over the origin of the Plates. Those who support the "Sideral
  artefacts" theory are often accused of materialist heresy.
* **Interpretive Conflict:** The Church condemns those who interpret the Plates as evidence of the Eleven's non-divine
  nature, preferring the "gift of Othmar" narrative.

## Sayings and Culture

The name of the sacred book ("The Chronicles of the Walking") permeates everyday speech and curses:

* **To sin:** People say *"Leaving the Walking"* (straying from the path).
* **Heresy:** Those who misinterpret the text are called a *"Deviant"* (one who has deviated from the road).
* **Blessing:** *"May your step be sure"* or *"May Othmar guide your journey"*.
* **Death:** *"They have finished their Walking"*.
