# Biosphere of Ondgard

## An Exuberant Garden

Ondgard's biosphere is the result of a unique balance between favourable physical conditions and extreme chemistry. If
the geosphere provides stability and the ecosphere the climate, the biosphere is the explosion of life that follows:
**a world where vegetation dominates with a luxuriance unknown to Earth, and where fauna has conquered the sky in
spectacular fashion.**

---

## Biochemical Foundations

### Chemistry of Life: Carbon and Water

As on Earth, life on Ondgard is based on:

* **Carbon (C):** The building element, capable of forming complex and stable chains.
* **Water (H₂O):** The universal solvent, abundant in the oceans (65% of the surface) and in the atmosphere (1% water
  vapour).
* **Nitrogen (N):** Essential for proteins and nucleic acids, abundant in the atmosphere (67%).
* **Oxygen (O₂):** Breathable (partial pressure 0.21 atm), enabling efficient energy metabolism.

### CO₂:

The atmosphere contains **2% CO₂ (20,000 ppm)**, a level 50 times higher than modern Earth.

* **For Complex Fauna:** Animal life has adapted with:
    * **Enhanced respiratory systems:** More efficient lungs, reduced respiratory rate.
    * **Blood buffers:** Blood with a greater capacity to neutralise acidity (elevated bicarbonate).

* **For Flora:** It is an **absolute paradise**. Photosynthesis on Earth is limited by CO₂ scarcity (400 ppm). At
  20,000 ppm, plants grow with unprecedented speed and biomass:
    * **Accelerated Growth:** Plants reach colossal dimensions in short timeframes.
    * **Water Efficiency:** They can partially close their stomata (leaf pores), reducing water loss while maintaining
      high photosynthesis.
    * **Vegetative Dominance:** Total plant biomass is estimated at **3–5 times** Earth's.

## Adapted Photosynthesis: The Red-Purple World

### The Stellar Spectrum Problem

The star Kepler (K0IV) emits light shifted toward **red and near infrared (NIR)**, poor in blue and green. Terrestrial
chlorophyll (which absorbs blue and red, reflecting green) would be inefficient.

### Alternative Pigments

Ondgard's plants have evolved different photosynthetic pigments:

* **Bacteriochlorophyll d/e:** Absorbs strongly in the near infrared (700–1,000 nm), allowing them to exploit the
  abundant radiation of the orange star.
* **Phycoerythrin and Phycocyanin:** Accessory pigments that absorb green and yellow, maximising the capture of
  available light.
* **Extended Carotenoids:** Protect against free radicals and absorb in the green-yellow range.

### Appearance of the Vegetation

The result is a flora of alien colours:

* **Primary Forests:** **Dark red-purple** foliage, almost **black-violet** in shaded areas.
* **Grasslands:** Shades of **garnet, burgundy and bronze**.
* **Aquatic Plants:** **Deep violet, blue-black** in deep waters.
* **Reflections:** Under Kepler's orange light, vegetation appears **incandescent crimson** at sunset, creating
  landscapes of fire.

> [!NOTE]
> **Exception: Surface Algae**
> In illuminated coastal waters, some algae retain green pigments (chlorophyll a/b) to exploit light reflected from
> the blue sky. They create emerald-green patches in contrast with the deep violet of deeper algae.

## Flora: The Vegetal Giants

### Favourable Conditions

The combination of **high CO₂ (2%)**, **reduced gravity (0.8 g)**, **dense atmosphere (1.75×)** and **stable climate**
creates the perfect environment for gigantic plants.

#### 1. Colossal Forests

Ondgard's forests are living cathedrals, with trees that dwarf terrestrial dimensions.

* **Height:** The largest trees reach **150–200 metres** (compared to the 115 m of the terrestrial Sequoia). Reduced
  gravity permits taller structures without collapse.
* **Massive Trunks:** Diameters of 15–25 metres at the base. The wood is dense but reinforced with interwoven
  cellulose fibres and rigid resins.
* **Layered Canopies:** Forests have 4–5 canopy levels, creating complex vertical ecosystems.
* **Shallow Roots:** The thin crust and hydrated mantle favour roots that spread horizontally rather than deeply,
  forming symbiotic networks with giant mycorrhizal fungi.

**Examples of Forest Biomes:**

* **Equatorial Rainforests:** Impenetrable jungles with trees 120–150 m tall, lianas as thick as trunks, and an
  understorey in perpetual shadow. Constant humidity (80–95%), temperature 22–32 °C.
* **Temperate Forests of the "Golden Belt":** Broadleaf and giant conifer woods with red-purple canopies that turn
  bronze in autumn. Ideal climate (14–26 °C), moderate rainfall.
* **Boreal Forests:** Dark conifers (black-violet) 80–100 m tall, adapted to the cold (0–18 °C) and the long winter
  night. Slow growth but enormous biomass.

#### 2. Grasslands and Savannas

In less rainy zones (subtropical and continental), grasslands replace forests.

* **Giant Grasses:** Graminoids 3–5 metres tall (resembling terrestrial bamboo), with silica-reinforced hollow stems.
* **Colour:** Red-garnet, burgundy, with golden or crimson inflorescences.
* **Biomass:** Extremely rapid growth (maturation in 2–3 months thanks to elevated CO₂). The grasslands are "vegetal
  oceans" undulating in the dense wind.
* **Windy Savannas:** Isolated trees (baobab-like) with broad, flat canopies adapted to constant winds. Low, resilient
  grasses.

#### 3. Aquatic Vegetation

Oceans and inland seas teem with plant life.

* **Giant Kelp Forests:** Algae 100–150 metres tall, anchored to rocky seabeds and held up by gas-filled bladders.
  They form three-dimensional underwater forests, habitat for thousands of species.
* **Submerged Meadows:** Marine phanerogams (similar to Posidonia) covering vast coastal seabeds, oxygenating the
  water and stabilising sediments.
* **Abundant Phytoplankton:** Unicellular algae (diatoms, dinoflagellates) forming the base of the oceanic food chain.
  Concentration 2–3 times higher than on Earth, making coastal waters turbid and rich.

#### 4. Extreme Adaptations

* **Giant Carnivorous Plants:** In nitrogen-poor zones (peat bogs, plateaux), carnivorous plants 5–10 metres tall
  capture insects and small vertebrates with pitfall traps or adhesive tentacles.
* **Flying Plants (Aerophytes):** Small epiphytic plants with gas-filled bladders (hydrogen produced by fermentation)
  that float in the dense air, dispersing seeds via the wind.
* **Polar Succulents:** In the tundra, succulent plants that accumulate water and antifreeze (glycerol) to survive
  winter temperatures of −30 °C.

## Fauna: The Realm of Flight

### Favourable Conditions for Flight

The combination of **reduced gravity (0.8 g)** and **dense atmosphere (1.75×)** makes flight extremely efficient:

* **Increased Lift:** Dense air generates greater lift at the same speed. Wings can be smaller or support heavier
  bodies.
* **Reduced Weight:** The 0.8 g gravity reduces the effective weight of organisms by 20%.
* **Easy Take-Off:** Even large animals can take flight with little effort.

**Result:** Flying fauna dominates the biosphere, with unprecedented size and diversity.

#### 1. Flying Megafauna

The equivalents of birds, bats and insects reach colossal dimensions.

* **Giant Birds:**
    * **Wingspan:** 15–25 metres (compared to the 3.5 m of the terrestrial Albatross).
    * **Weight:** Up to 200–300 kg (compared to the 15 kg of the largest terrestrial flying bird, the prehistoric
      Argentavis).
    * **Ecology:** Apex predators that hunt in flocks, soaring for hours on constant winds. They nest in the crowns of
      giant trees or on cliffs.
    * **Appearance:** Dark plumage (black, blue-violet, red) for camouflage against the blue-grey sky. Robust beaks,
      powerful talons.

* **Giant Flying Insects:**
    * **Dragonflies:** 80–120 cm wingspan, aerial predators that hunt other insects.
    * **Butterflies/Moths:** Wings 50–70 cm wide, with iridescent colours (electric blue, emerald green, gold) for
      communication and camouflage.

* **Flying Reptiles (Draconiformes):**
    * Creatures resembling pterosaurs or dragons, with wing membranes supported by elongated fingers.
    * **Size:** 10–20 metre wingspan, 100–200 kg weight.
    * **Ecology:** Thermal gliders exploiting updrafts. Some are piscivorous (hunting at sea), others terrestrial
      carnivores.
    * **Peculiarity:** Some have developed gas bladders (methane) in the body to further reduce weight, becoming
      near-"living dirigibles".

#### 2. Terrestrial Fauna

Gigantism is the dominant trend on Ondgard, but **not an exclusive one**. Alongside the megafauna there exists a rich
understory of small and medium-sized animals occupying complementary ecological niches — just as on Earth elephants
coexist with mice, and eagles with sparrows.

* **Mega-Herbivores:**
    * Creatures resembling sauropods or elephants, 8–12 metres tall, browsing the canopies of giant trees.
    * **Adaptations:** Long necks, complex digestive systems for processing cellulose-rich vegetation. Slow metabolisms
      to tolerate elevated CO₂.
    * **Locomotion:** Columnar legs, slow gait. Reduced gravity permits more massive bodies without skeletal collapse.

* **Terrestrial Predators:**
    * Medium-sized carnivores (resembling large felids or theropods), agile and fast.
    * **Hunting:** Ambushes in forests, pursuits across grasslands. Some hunt in packs.

* **Small and Medium Fauna:**
    * Gigantism does not eliminate smaller wildlife: mammals resembling hares, foxes, badgers and rodents thrive in the
      undergrowth, grasslands and underground burrows. Medium-to-small birds (analogous to pheasants, partridges and
      corvids) occupy the lower canopy niches and the forest floor.
    * **Ecological role:** These animals form the base of the food chain for medium-sized predators, disperse seeds,
      control insect populations, and are the most common prey for humanoid hunters.
    * **Size:** Slightly larger than their terrestrial counterparts (an effect of low gravity), but they remain animals
      that can be carried and hunted with conventional means.

* **Arboreal Fauna:**
    * Primates, marsupials, reptiles living in the canopies of giant trees, moving between canopy levels.
    * **Adaptations:** Prehensile limbs, prehensile tails, gliding membranes (similar to terrestrial flying squirrels
      but more developed).

#### 3. Aquatic Fauna

The oceans are rich with life, with complex food chains.

* **Marine Megafauna:**
    * Creatures resembling whales, 30–50 metres long, filtering the abundant phytoplankton.
    * Apex predators (resembling orcas or mosasaurs) hunting fish and giant cephalopods.

* **Fish and Cephalopods:**
    * Enormous diversity, with forms adapted to every ecological niche.
    * **Giant Cephalopods:** Octopuses and squid with arms 10–15 metres long, intelligent and camouflaged.

* **Marine Reptiles:**
    * Creatures resembling plesiosaurs or ichthyosaurs, breathing air but hunting underwater.

## Biogeochemical Cycles

### Accelerated Carbon Cycle

Elevated CO₂ accelerates the carbon cycle:

1. **Massive Photosynthesis:** Plants fix enormous quantities of CO₂ (3–5 times Earth's rate).
2. **Plant Biomass:** Organic matter accumulates rapidly (forests, grasslands, oceans).
3. **Decomposition:** Fungi and bacteria decompose the biomass, releasing CO₂ and methane (CH₄).
4. **Geological Sequestration:** Some carbon is buried in sediments (coal, peat, carbonates), removing it from the
   atmosphere for millions of years.
5. **Volcanism:** Volcanoes release CO₂ from the mantle, closing the cycle.

**Equilibrium:** The system is in dynamic balance, with volcanism compensating geological sequestration, maintaining
CO₂ at 2%.

### Nitrogen Cycle

Atmospheric nitrogen (67%) is fixed by:

* **Symbiotic Bacteria:** Living in plant roots (similar to terrestrial rhizobia), converting N₂ into ammonia (NH₃).
* **Lightning:** Shinosa's electromagnetic storms ionise nitrogen, forming oxides (NOₓ) that precipitate with rain.
* **Decomposition:** Nitrifying and denitrifying bacteria recycle organic nitrogen.

### Oxygen Cycle

* **Production:** Massive photosynthesis produces excess O₂.
* **Consumption:** Animal respiration, decomposition, mineral oxidation (iron, sulphur).
* **Equilibrium:** The O₂ concentration (15% vol, 0.21 atm) is stable, maintained by the balance between production
  and consumption.

## Adaptations to the 32-Hour Rotation

### Extended Circadian Rhythms

Organisms have developed 32-hour biological cycles:

* **Plants:** Stomatal opening/closing, flowering and pollen release follow the 32-hour day/night cycle.
* **Diurnal Animals:** Active for 16 hours of light, resting for 16 hours of darkness. Some have developed mid-day
  "siestas" to avoid the afternoon heat peak.
* **Nocturnal Animals:** Hunt during the 16 hours of darkness, exploiting the light of the moons (Astro and Shinosa)
  and the auroras.
* **Crepuscular Animals:** Active at dawn and dusk — transition periods that last longer (2–3 hours) because of the
  slow rotation.

### Thermoregulation Strategies

The day/night thermal excursion (8–20 °C depending on the zone) requires adaptations:

* **Warm-Blooded Fauna (Endotherms):** Maintain constant body temperature, but with slowed metabolisms to tolerate
  CO₂. Insulating fur/feathers for the night.
* **Cold-Blooded Fauna (Ectotherms):** Reptiles and amphibians that bask in the sun during the day and shelter in
  insulated dens at night. Some enter nocturnal torpor.
* **Behavioural Strategies:** Vertical migration (arboreal animals descend to ground level at night where it is
  warmer), aggregation (compact groups to conserve heat).

## Effects of the Moons on the Biosphere

### Astro: The Monthly Rhythm

Astro's 30-day orbit influences:

* **Reproduction:** Many marine species synchronise egg-laying with Astro's phases (higher tides = larval dispersal).
* **Migrations:** Birds and fish follow monthly cycles to exploit tidal currents.

### Shinosa: Magnetic Chaos

The close passage every 45 hours creates disturbances:

* **Disorientation:** Migratory animals that rely on the magnetic field (birds, sea turtles) are temporarily confused.
* **Auroras and Nocturnal Activity:** Spectacular auroras create "lit nights", altering nocturnal behaviours. Predators
  and prey alike adapt to this intermittent light.
* **Electromagnetic Storms:** Clear-sky lightning can ignite fires in dry grasslands, creating cycles of ecological
  renewal (like natural terrestrial wildfires).

## Biodiversity and Biomass

### Comparative Estimates (vs Earth)

* **Total Plant Biomass:** **3–5 times** greater (thanks to elevated CO₂, stable climate, larger surface area).
* **Total Animal Biomass:** **1.5–2 times** greater (higher primary productivity = more herbivores and carnivores).
* **Number of Species:** Estimated at **15–25 million** (versus 8–10 million on Earth), thanks to the larger surface,
  habitat variety and climatic stability.

### Biodiversity Hotspots

* **Equatorial Forests:** Maximum diversity, with thousands of species per km².
* **Coral Reefs (equivalents):** Reefs built by calcareous organisms (corals, coralline algae) in warm, shallow seas.
* **Inland Seas:** Semi-enclosed ecosystems with endemic species.
* **Volcanic Islands:** Evolutionary laboratories with unique species (adaptive radiation).

## Habitability for Humanoid Life

### Favourable Factors

* **Breathable Oxygen:** Partial pressure identical to Earth's (0.21 atm).
* **Mild Temperature:** 14–26 °C range in the "Golden Belt", ideal for life.
* **Abundant Water:** Oceans, rivers, regular rainfall.
* **Food:** Abundant vegetation and fauna, easily hunted/cultivated.
* **Tolerable Gravity:** 0.8 g is comfortable, reducing fatigue and joint problems.

### Challenges

* **Elevated CO₂ (2%):** Requires physiological adaptation (enhanced respiratory systems, blood buffers).
* **32-Hour Day:** Requires circadian rhythm adaptation.
* **Dangerous Fauna:** Giant predators (birds, flying reptiles, terrestrial carnivores) demand precautions.

## Conclusion: A Living and Lush World

Ondgard's biosphere is a triumph of life. The unique physical and chemical conditions have created an ecosystem where:

* **Vegetation reigns supreme**, with colossal red-purple forests covering the continents and giant algae filling the
  oceans.
* **The sky is the domain of flying fauna**, with gigantic creatures soaring on the dense, constant winds.
* **Life is abundant and diverse**, with biomass and biodiversity exceeding Earth's.

It is a world that **breathes**, **pulses** and **grows** with overwhelming vitality. An exuberant garden beneath an
orange sun, where nature has pushed the limits of biology beyond all terrestrial imagination.
