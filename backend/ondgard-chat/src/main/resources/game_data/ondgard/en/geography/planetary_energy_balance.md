# Energy Balance and Climate of Ondgard

## Equilibrium Overview

Ondgard's habitability rests on a fortunate equation: **a slightly underpowered stellar "engine" balanced by a highly
efficient atmospheric "blanket".**

If Ondgard had Earth's atmosphere, it would be a frozen world. If it received Earth's irradiance, it would be an
infernal greenhouse. The current combination creates a climate that is **mild, stable and remarkably clear**.

---

## 1. Energy Input: The Cold Sun

The star Kepler (Orange Subgiant) delivers less raw energy than the Sun, but with a different quality.

* **Solar Constant at Ondgard:** ~1,225 W/m² (90% of Earth's).
* **Equilibrium Temperature (Without Atmosphere):** −25 °C (248 K). Without a greenhouse effect, the planet would be
  frozen.
* **Light Spectrum:** Low in UV (harmful ultraviolet) and rich in **Near Infrared (NIR)**.
    * *Consequence:* The ocean and ground absorb heat very efficiently, since water absorbs infrared strongly. Heating
      is "deep" and uniform.

---

## 2. The "Clear Sky": Albedo and Transparency

Despite its dense atmosphere (1.4 atm), Ondgard's sky is **not** a perpetual haze. On the contrary, atmospheric physics
favours incredibly clear skies for extended periods.

### Why Are the Skies Clear?

1. **Slow and Stable Circulation:** The 32-hour rotation and weak Coriolis force create gigantic, stationary
   high-pressure cells (anticyclones). Within these zones, air descends (subsidence), inhibiting cloud formation and
   ensuring crystalline skies for weeks.
2. **Efficient Precipitation:** When clouds do form (in large frontal systems), the dense atmosphere promotes droplet
   coalescence. "It rains well and all at once." Moisture is discharged efficiently to the ground rather than lingering
   as perpetual haze.
3. **Absence of Fine Particulates:** Constant winds and widespread vegetation reduce airborne dust.
4. **Global Albedo (~0.28):** Slightly lower than Earth's (0.30). Dark oceans absorb the star's infrared well, and the
   lower mean cloud cover (concentrated in well-defined bands) allows light to reach the surface.

---

## 3. The Greenhouse Effect

To go from −25 °C (equilibrium temperature) to a pleasant average, the system must retain a great deal of heat.

* **Required Thermal Delta:** +42 °C (Earth: +33 °C).
* **The Agents:**
    * **CO₂ at 2% (20,000 ppm):** This is the main driver. At 1.4 atm pressure, the CO₂ absorption lines "broaden"
      (pressure broadening), making the gas even more effective at blocking heat escape into space.
    * **Krypton and Density:** Although Krypton is not a greenhouse gas, it increases air density. Denser air transfers
      heat more effectively by conduction and convection, reducing nocturnal ground cooling.

**Result:** The greenhouse effect exactly compensates for the 10% solar-light deficit, bringing the global mean
temperature to **~17 °C** (similar to or slightly above present-day Earth, but better distributed).

---

## 4. Temperatures and Habitability

Thanks to thermal inertia, there are no violent extremes. It is a "warm-temperate" planet.

### Temperature Range (Day/Night)

The thermal flywheel of the oceans and dense atmosphere keeps the excursion contained.

| Zone                            | Min. Temp. (Deep Night) | Max. Temp. (Afternoon) | Sensation                                                               |
|:--------------------------------|:------------------------|:-----------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Equatorial**                  | 22 °C                   | 32 °C                  | Hot and humid, but tolerable thanks to constant winds.                  |
| **Subtropical ("Golden Belt")** | **14 °C**               | **26 °C**              | **Eternal spring.** Ideal for human life.                               |
| **Upper Temperate**             | 5 °C                    | 18 °C                  | Cool, similar to Atlantic or British climate, but with more gold light. |
| **Polar (Summer)**              | 0 °C                    | 10 °C                  | Liveable tundra.                                                        |
| **Polar (Winter)**              | −30 °C                  | −15 °C                 | Cold, but rarely as extreme as Siberia or Antarctica.                   |

### Notes on Human Perception

* **Light:** The orange-gold light is less "glaring" than white sunlight. Clear skies appear deep blue, almost
  aqua-green toward the horizon (Rayleigh scattering in a dense atmosphere with redder incident light).
* **Heat:** Warmth feels "close to the skin". The abundance of infrared means the sun heats skin and clothing very
  quickly, even when the air is cool. It is the sensation of a "constant spring mountain day in the sun".

---

## 5. Conclusion: An Optical Paradise

* There are **Major Disturbances** (overcast skies, slow and steady rains) lasting 2–3 days.
* Followed by **Long High-Pressure Periods** (10–15 days) in which the dense air stabilises, moisture precipitates
  and the sky becomes a crystalline dome perfect for astronomical observation (or navigation).
* There is no "milky haze" typical of humid worlds; instead there is a sharp contrast between the deep blue of the sky
  and the gold of the star.
