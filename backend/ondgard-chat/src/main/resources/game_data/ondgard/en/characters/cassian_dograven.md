# Duke Cassian of Dograven

**Title:** Duke of Dograven, Lord of Dorghast
**Race:** Human
**Age:** 52 years
**Seat:** Dorghast

---

## Appearance

A man who wears the weight of his position on his face. Cassian has the groomed appearance of an imperial nobleman —
Voth's culture has permeated Dograven for generations — but his eyes betray a permanent anxiety. Hair grey prematurely,
hands that never stay still during audiences.

## Political Profile

Cassian inherited a duchy that his ancestors kept independent for three centuries through a single talent: **diplomatic
survival**. Today that talent is no longer enough.

To the north, the Empire of Voth treats Dograven as a de facto vassal: imperial legions cross ducal territory as though
it were their own province, tribute demands grow every year, and the imperial "advisors" at court are poorly disguised
spies.

To the south, the Kingdom of Audrock advances. Every year the Duchy's southern borders shrink — not through open
invasion, but through steady erosion: border tribes swearing allegiance to Audrock, trade routes shifting, southern
watchtowers finding Elissa's soldiers camped across the valley.

Cassian plays the only card at his disposal: **indispensability**. As long as Dograven remains a useful buffer for both
powers, neither has an interest in destroying it. But the Duke knows this calculus has an expiry date — the day when
Voth or Audrock decide that direct control of the mountain passes is worth more than keeping a weak ally.

## Public Reputation

His subjects respect him for the stability he has guaranteed, but perceive him as a man **in a trap**. Merchants
appreciate him because he keeps the routes open. Voth considers him a useful servant. Audrock considers him a temporary
obstacle.

## Notable Traits

* A diplomat by necessity, not by vocation — he would rather fight but knows he cannot afford a war
* Intimidated by both neighbours, but clear-headed enough not to side openly with either
* Skilled at reading the balance of power and at buying time
* Suffers the humiliation of an independence that is increasingly a fiction
